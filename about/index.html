Most advisors leave the day a deal closes. I've spent my career on both sides of that day. I've built the models, run the diligence, and negotiated the seller notes. Then I stayed for the hard part: integrating the business, getting its books closed every month, and making the numbers in the investment memo actually show up.

Much of my work has been acquiring local, owner-operated companies. Many had been around for 20 years or more, with great customers, loyal crews, and almost no financial infrastructure. I've sat across the table from those owners at the LOI, and I've been the one rebuilding their finances after we closed: building a finance function from the ground up, standing up the monthly close, and integrating each new business into it.

That work taught me two things. First, first-time buyers almost never lose money because they picked the wrong company. They lose it on add-backs that don't hold up, working capital pegs set too low, and debt that only works if nothing goes wrong. Second, the best local businesses are often worth far more than their owners realize, but messy books make buyers discount them.

Why I started ThriveStart

Private equity firms have deal teams, operating partners, and finance staff. The independent sponsor closing a first acquisition doesn't. Neither does the family business doing $15 million a year with a bookkeeper and QuickBooks. Both need senior judgment, and neither can justify a $300K CFO.

ThriveStart closes that gap. Independent sponsors get a deal partner from LOI to close and through the first 100 days. Established owners get a finance function that runs itself, plus a monthly sit-down with someone who knows what a buyer, a lender, or a bank will ask.

How I use AI

I've built AI into how I run deals and finance, not as a pitch. On acquisitions, I use AI-assisted workflows to rebuild seller P&Ls from the general ledger, test add-backs, analyze customer and billing data, and assemble diligence findings in days instead of weeks. In finance operations, the same approach drives the monthly close, management reporting, and integration tracking.

The point isn't the technology. It's that the grunt work gets faster and cheaper, so my time goes into what software can't do: judging whether a deal is worth doing, negotiating terms that protect your cash, and telling you the hard truth when you need it.
