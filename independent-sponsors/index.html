The first-deal problem

Everyone at the table has done this before. Except you.

The seller has a broker. The lender has a credit committee. Your investors have seen a hundred deals. You have a signed LOI, a 60-day exclusivity clock, and a lot riding on getting it right.

Most first-time sponsors don't lose deals because they picked the wrong company. They lose money on add-backs that don't survive diligence, a peg set too low, a seller note that strangles cash, and a first year with no real finance function.

Where first deals go wrong

$400K of “owner add-backs” that are really a GM's salary

A working capital peg that hands the seller a month of cash

Debt service that only works if nothing goes wrong

A bookkeeper who quits the week after close

FAQ

Questions sponsors ask us

Do you replace a QoE provider?

Usually not. Most lenders require a third-party QoE. We make sure you walk into it knowing what it will find, scope it so you aren't overpaying, and turn its findings into price and structure changes.

Can you help before I have an LOI?

Yes. We screen targets, sanity-check asking prices, and help you write an LOI with the right structure from day one. Fixing a bad LOI is much harder than writing a good one.

Will lenders and investors take your work seriously?

That's the point. Our models and packages are built the way credit committees and LPs expect to see them, which helps a first-time sponsor look like an experienced one.

What happens after close?

We can stay on as your finance function: monthly close, 13-week cash, KPI pack, and investor reporting, automated so it doesn't depend on one bookkeeper. Many sponsors keep us through year one.

How do you price a deal engagement?

Scoped to the deal, not open-ended hours. You'll get a fixed proposal after the teardown call, backed by our 30-day money-back guarantee.
